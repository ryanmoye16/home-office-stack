Buying Guide — Mice

Best Mouse for a Mac

A Mac buyer does not need a mouse whose listing says “for Mac”: all four mice we review list macOS in their compatibility, and the standard versions serve a Mac and a mixed desk alike. What a Mac buyer does need is three checks the compatibility line hides. The connection: every one of these mice pairs over Bluetooth, the native path on a MacBook whose only ports are USB-C, while the receiver in each box is USB-A and needs an adapter or a hub to reach that MacBook. The system and its software: one of the four carries a macOS version floor the other three do not record, and the customisation layer lives in Logi Options+ on macOS. And then shape — because once the Mac layer narrows the field, the hand still decides.

Check one: the connection on a USB-C Mac — Bluetooth is the native path

Start with the port you do not have to use. All four mice here connect over Bluetooth Low Energy, and on a current MacBook — a machine whose ports are USB-C only — that is the native path: pair once in macOS, and the mouse arrives with no adapter, no hub and no port given up. For most Mac desks this check ends there. The receiver conversation below matters to readers whose desk gives them a reason to use one: a machine where Bluetooth is managed or unreliable, or a desktop Mac where a receiver has simply proved steadier.

Where a receiver is the route, read the box honestly. The receivers included with these mice are USB-A: a Logi Bolt receiver with the Lift, the MX Master 3S and the ERGO M575S, and the older Logitech Unifying receiver with the MX Vertical. On a MacBook with USB-C-only ports, none of them plugs straight in — each needs an adapter or a USB-C hub, and our MacBook hub guide covers the hubs we review. Bolt and Unifying do not pair with each other, so a Bolt receiver in a hub does nothing for the Unifying MX Vertical, and the vertical's receiver does nothing for the three Bolt mice. On an older Mac with a USB-A port, each included receiver plugs straight in, as our keyboard guide for a Mac notes for the same receiver families.

One mouse here keeps a wired door open. The MX Vertical also works wired over its included USB-C cable, and it stays usable while it charges. No other mouse here has a wired mode: the Lift and the ERGO M575S run on AA batteries with nothing to plug in, and the MX Master 3S's recorded connection set is Bluetooth and its Bolt receiver. If a cable fallback matters to your Mac setup, that is a genuine split, not a footnote.

Check two: the macOS version, the software, and the versions not covered here

The version floor splits the four, and it splits them unevenly. The ERGO M575S carries the newest operating-system floor of the four: its recorded compatibility is macOS 12 or later, alongside iPadOS 15 or later and Android 12 or later, with basic functions without the app outside Windows and macOS. An older Mac running a system below macOS 12 cannot use the M575S as catalogued — the single hard exclusion in this guide. The other three list plain macOS compatibility with no version floor recorded: the MX Master 3S, the Lift and the MX Vertical each record “macOS” in their compatibility lines, and this guide claims nothing more precise than that.

The software layer is where a Mac mouse earns its buttons. Customisation on these mice runs in Logi Options+ on macOS, and the recorded counts are part of the pick: 4 customisable buttons on the Lift, customisable buttons on the MX Master 3S alongside its gesture button and thumb wheel, 4 customisable buttons on the MX Vertical, and 3 customisable buttons on the ERGO M575S. Logitech Flow cross-computer control — pointer and copy-paste travelling between computers — is listed for the Lift, the MX Master 3S and the MX Vertical, the same three that switch across up to three devices with Easy-Switch. It is not listed for the ERGO M575S, and neither is Easy-Switch: the trackball connects by its included Bolt receiver or Bluetooth.

Finally, the version question, stated before a listing page raises it. Logitech sells a Lift for Mac and an MX Master 3S for Mac version. Those are different listings — different models from the standard versions reviewed here — and we have not reviewed them, so nothing in this guide, no figure and no verdict, carries across to them. The standard versions covered here list macOS and serve Mac and mixed desks alike — one mouse for a Mac and a Windows machine both — which the Mac-only variants by design do not offer. The MX Vertical has no variant at all, and the ERGO M575S variants are only colourway and UK listings.

Check three: shape still decides — the Mac layer narrows, the hand chooses

Run the first two checks and the field narrows — Bluetooth everywhere, one version floor, one wired fallback — but nothing in them tells your hand what to hold all day. The MX Master 3S is the sculpted conventional body: palm-down, scroll-heavy, with a sensor that tracks where the others cannot. The Lift stands small-to-medium hands up at a 57-degree handshake angle. The MX Vertical stands the same 57 degrees in a larger body, rechargeable. And the ERGO M575S removes movement from the question entirely: the body never travels, your thumb rolls the ball, and the mouse needs only its own footprint. Read the four picks below by shape, with the Mac checks as the filter you apply on the way in.

When none of these is the right buy

Handedness limits the whole guide first: all four mice are right-hand in the listings reviewed — the MX Master 3S and the MX Vertical have no left-hand version, and the Lift's left-hand version is a different listing. A left-handed Mac reader is outside what these picks cover. The second limit is the version gate: a Mac below macOS 12 still has three picks here, but the stationary trackball is closed to it. And some Mac desks honestly want the pointer built for the platform — a trackpad-centred setup, judged on gesture support and how it sits beside a MacBook keyboard. We have not reviewed Apple's pointing devices, so that category is named here as criteria only, with no verdict; if gestures are the point of your desk, shop it on its own terms.

Before you buy: the two-minute Mac test

Two minutes at the Mac settles which version of this guide you are shopping in. First, the version test: open the Apple menu's About This Mac and read the macOS version. Twelve or later keeps all four picks open; below it, cross the trackball off before its stationary case persuades you. Second, the port test: count the Mac's USB-C ports and what already lives in them. Bluetooth pairing needs none of them, which is why it is the default route; if you plan on a receiver, name the adapter or hub it will live in, using our MacBook hub guide, and remember Bolt and Unifying do not share. Third, the hand test: measure nothing, just notice the strain, if any — palm-down twist points at the verticals, arm travel points at the trackball, and no strain at all points at the sculpted default. Then buy that mouse, and pair it on the day it arrives, inside the return window.

Mac mouse questions, answered honestly

Do I need a mouse made 'for Mac' to use it with a Mac?

No. All four mice we review list macOS in their recorded compatibility, so the standard versions covered here work with a Mac and with a mixed Mac and Windows desk. Logitech also sells separate 'for Mac' versions of two of them — the Lift for Mac and the MX Master 3S for Mac — but those are different listings, with different models, that we have not reviewed, and nothing in this guide carries across to them. The MX Vertical has no 'for Mac' variant, and the ERGO M575S variants are only colourway and UK listings.

How do these mice connect to a MacBook that only has USB-C ports?

Over Bluetooth Low Energy, which is the native path on a USB-C MacBook: all four mice pair that way, with no port used at all. Their included receivers are USB-A — Logi Bolt on the Lift, the MX Master 3S and the ERGO M575S, and Unifying on the MX Vertical — so on a MacBook with USB-C-only ports a receiver needs an adapter or a USB-C hub, and Bolt and Unifying receivers do not pair with each other. The MX Vertical is the one exception to the wireless-only pattern: it also works wired over its included USB-C cable, and stays usable while it charges.

Will all four of these mice work on an older Mac?

Three list plain macOS compatibility with no version floor recorded — the MX Master 3S, the Lift and the MX Vertical — so this guide records no macOS version that rules them out. The fourth is different: the ERGO M575S carries the newest operating-system floor of the four, macOS 12 or later, alongside iPadOS 15 or later and Android 12 or later, with basic functions without the app outside Windows and macOS. An older Mac running a system below macOS 12 cannot use the M575S as catalogued, so check your macOS version before choosing the trackball.

What does Logi Options+ do on a Mac, and which of these mice support Logitech Flow?

On macOS, Logi Options+ carries the customisation: 4 customisable buttons on the Lift, customisable buttons plus a gesture button and a thumb wheel on the MX Master 3S, 4 customisable buttons on the MX Vertical, and 3 customisable buttons on the ERGO M575S. Logitech Flow cross-computer control — moving the pointer, and copy-paste, between computers — is listed for the Lift, the MX Master 3S and the MX Vertical, which each switch across up to three devices. It is not listed for the ERGO M575S, and neither is Easy-Switch or any multi-device switching: the trackball connects by its included Bolt receiver or Bluetooth.

Which of these mice is best for a Mac?

For most Mac desks, the Logitech MX Master 3S: a sculpted conventional shape, a Darkfield sensor that tracks even on glass, MagSpeed and thumb wheels, up to three devices with Flow, and a rechargeable battery rated at up to 70 days. Shape can overrule it: small-to-medium hands wanting a 57-degree vertical angle take the Logitech Lift, larger hands the Logitech MX Vertical — which also works wired while charging — and a desk with no room for mouse travel takes the Logitech ERGO M575S trackball, conditional on macOS 12 or later.

Bottom Line

Run the connection and the version first — then let the hand choose

Ask first how the mouse reaches the Mac — Bluetooth, natively, for all four, with USB-A receivers that need an adapter or hub on a USB-C MacBook — then whether the Mac's version clears the one floor in the guide. For most Mac desks the answer is the Logitech MX Master 3S: sculpted, glass-tracking, Flow across machines, and the fullest Options+ kit on macOS. Where small-to-medium hands want the 57-degree angle and a two-year AA battery, the Logitech Lift is the pick on the same Bluetooth-and-Bolt footing. Where larger hands want that angle with a four-month rechargeable battery and a cable it can work through while charging, the Logitech MX Vertical is the pick — with its Unifying receiver stated plainly.
