Buying Guide — Smart Plugs

Best Smart Plug for Holiday Lights

The short answer first: holiday lights are a scheduling job before they are anything else. The lights should arrive as the room darkens, leave before the household is asleep, and keep a believable pattern on the nights nobody is home. This guide runs the three plugs we've reviewed through the seasonal checks in order: a schedule that tracks the dark, platform and voice in a full house, and the indoor and load limits. Our general smart plug guide ranks platform-first and our energy monitoring guide ranks monitoring-first — here, the schedule decides.

Check one: a schedule that tracks the dark

A fixed clock time is the wrong shape for holiday lights, because the dark does not keep office hours. A display set for a fixed early-evening hour is lit while the room is still bright in the first weeks of the season and late a few weeks later, when that hour falls well after sunset. Read every scheduling claim by asking two questions: can it follow the dark, and can it guarantee an off — because a display nobody remembers to switch off is the season's most ordinary waste, repeated nightly.

Only one of the three plugs tracks the dark as a recorded feature. The Kasa EP25 offers sunrise and sunset scheduling in the Kasa app, alongside custom schedules and timers, with no hub and no subscription. That is the differentiator on this page: set the lights to the sunset pattern once, and the on time moves as the evenings move. The same app carries its Away Mode and grouping, so several plugs on several displays can follow one pattern together.

The Tapo plug's answer is the guaranteed off. The Tapo P110M records scheduling in the Tapo app, and alongside it an Auto-Off and an Away Mode, both built in. Auto-Off matches the second half of this check: set how long the lights may run, and they cannot be left on all night by a household that went to bed and forgot them. Its schedule is set by the clock rather than by the sun — no sunset tracking is recorded for it — so the on time is yours to adjust as the season darkens, but the off is handled.

The Amazon plug schedules the way Alexa households already live. The Amazon Smart Plug schedules through routines in the Alexa app — the same routines an Alexa household already uses for lamps — so a holiday routine is one more entry in a familiar list. Its review records owners running holiday lights on this plug directly, alongside lamps, fans and coffee makers, so the use case is owner-reported rather than a seasonal guess. What routines do not record here is sunset tracking or an Away Mode of the plug's own; the schedule is a clock schedule, set and adjusted by hand.

Away Mode is the travelling half of this check. The holiday weeks are when houses empty, and a dark house announces itself — while lights on one rigid timer announce something almost as plain. The EP25 and the P110M both record an Away Mode, which varies the pattern so the home looks lived in rather than mechanically timed. The Amazon plug's review records the manual version: on and off control from the Alexa app when away from home. Any of the three keeps lights working while the household travels; the first two automate the irregularity that makes the pattern believable.

Check two: platform and voice in a full house

A holiday plug gets more voices thrown at it than any desk plug ever does. The rest of the year, a plug answers to whoever set it up. In the holiday season it answers to the whole house: a partner whose phone runs a different assistant, children home for the break, guests who expect to say a phrase at the room and have the tree respond. A plug only one person's assistant can reach turns everyone else into someone walking to a wall switch. Platform breadth is a convenience in a home office; at a family gathering it is the difference between a display the house shares and one a single person operates.

The three plugs split that breadth as widely as three plugs can. The Tapo P110M speaks Matter over Wi-Fi and works across Alexa, Google Assistant, Siri, SmartThings and Apple Home — the full spread. The Kasa EP25 is an Apple HomeKit plug over Wi-Fi — not Matter, with no Thread or Zigbee — and works with Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant and SmartThings, with Apple Home as its home ground. The Amazon Smart Plug is Alexa only, in Amazon's own wording built for exclusive use with Alexa, supporting no other assistants or hubs. That is a clean fit in an all-Alexa house, and a hard wall in a house that is not.

Choose for the house you will actually have in the room. If every voice is already Alexa, the Amazon plug's limit never binds and its simplicity is real. If the house mixes iPhones and Android phones, the EP25 covers the common ground and the P110M covers all of it, including a future platform change — Matter support means the plug survives the household switching ecosystems. One recorded practicality: the P110M pairs over Matter with Bluetooth for setup, and pairing works best with the plug close to its controller, so the first pairing goes easiest near the house's hub rather than at the far outlet behind the tree.

Check three: indoor and load honesty

Start with the limit nobody can schedule around: every plug here is rated 15A / 1800W. Load capacity never separates these picks, so there is no higher-rated option hiding elsewhere in this comparison. The figures that matter are the connected display's own stated draw, set against that ceiling, and the light string maker's own limit for the strings being connected. Stay within both. This page invents no draw figures for light strings, because strings differ and a count built on a guessed draw would be a fiction with consequences.

Each plug also controls exactly one outlet. One plug runs the one display plugged into it; a tree, a mantel run and a window display on separate outlets are separate plugs, each with its own schedule. Body size matters when plugs multiply: the EP25's slim 2.62 by 1.5 by 1.57 inch body stacks two plugs in one duplex outlet, the P110M's compact body is designed to avoid blocking the adjacent outlet though owners report two side by side can still feel tight, and the Amazon plug is the largest of the three at 3.2 by 1.5 by 2.2 inches and 3.1 ounces.

The indoor limit is the honest boundary of this whole page. The Amazon Smart Plug is recorded as indoor use only. For the other two, neither review records an outdoor rating — and the absence of a recorded rating is not a rating. So these picks are indoor picks: the tree, the mantel, the window display inside the glass. Outdoor strings need a plug rated for outdoor use. We have not reviewed an outdoor-rated smart plug, so this page names none — criteria only, honestly stated, rather than an indoor plug waved at an outdoor job.

Two smaller recorded limits finish the check. All three plugs are 2.4GHz Wi-Fi only, so the outlet's network has to offer a 2.4GHz band the plug can join — a 5GHz-only setup fits none of them — and the EP25's recorded setup pain point lives here, on band-steered mesh networks. Control itself has the standing limit recorded in the EP25's review: a plug can only control a device that resumes when power returns, the kind with a physical switch. Plain light strings are that kind of load by nature, but any display piece with its own power button deserves the ten-second test — unplug it while it is on, plug it back in, and see whether it resumes — before a schedule is built on it.

The method: setting up the schedule

Pair the plug where the pairing works, then move it to where the lights are. The P110M's Matter pairing works best with the plug close to its controller, so pair it in the controller's room, confirm it answers, and only then carry it to the outlet behind the tree. The EP25's struggle is the band-steered mesh network: join the 2.4GHz band during setup if pairing stalls. The Amazon plug's route is the simplest — Wi-Fi Simple Setup inside the Alexa app. Every plug here is 2.4GHz only, so the network check comes before the schedule.

Test the display's resume behaviour before automating it. A plug controls a device by cutting and restoring power, so the display has to come back on when the power does. Run the test on the exact strings going on the plug: switch the display on, unplug it, plug it back in, and watch whether it resumes lit. Pieces that resume get scheduled with confidence; a piece that returns dark, waiting for its own button, belongs on a different arrangement — no schedule on any plug here can press that button for it.

Build the evening in the plug's own app, using the feature that plug actually records. On the EP25, set the sunset schedule in the Kasa app and let the on time travel with the dark; add grouping if several plugs run several displays. On the P110M, set the on time in the Tapo app and add Auto-Off as the backstop, sized to the household's real bedtime. On the Amazon plug, build the routine in the Alexa app. In each case, set the off with the same care as the on: the off is the half of the schedule that saves the waste, and the half nobody in a warm room remembers to do by hand.

Then set the away pattern before the first trip, not from the airport. Enable Away Mode on the EP25 or the P110M for travel stretches, and test it across one ordinary evening at home first. On the Amazon plug, the away tool is the Alexa app itself: its review records owners switching the plug from away from home, and the plug reconnecting after power or Wi-Fi outages — the behaviour a travelling owner relies on. The sequence is the same for every pick: pair near the controller, test the resume, build the evening, set the away pattern — once, before the season starts.

When none of these is the right buy

If the display that matters is outdoors, none of these is the right buy: the Amazon plug is indoor only, and neither other review records an outdoor rating, so that job needs an outdoor-rated plug — a category we have not reviewed yet. If the display spreads across many outlets, remember each of these plugs controls a single outlet, so the honest shape of that project is several plugs and a grouping feature, or a different class of control. And on a 5GHz-only network none of the three connects at all — the network, not the plug shelf, is where that project starts.

Holiday light questions, answered honestly

Can I use one of these smart plugs for outdoor holiday lights?

Not on the evidence we have. The Amazon Smart Plug is recorded as indoor use only, and neither the Tapo P110M nor the Kasa EP25 review records an outdoor rating, so none of the three can be recommended for an outdoor display. Outdoor strings need a plug that is itself rated for outdoor use — a rating none of these three carries in our reviews. We have not reviewed an outdoor-rated smart plug yet, so this page names no outdoor pick rather than guessing at one.

Can a smart plug turn my holiday lights on at sunset automatically?

One of the three does it as a recorded feature: the Kasa EP25 offers sunrise and sunset scheduling in the Kasa app, alongside custom schedules, so the lights can follow the dark rather than a fixed clock time. The Tapo P110M offers scheduling in the Tapo app with Auto-Off, and the Amazon Smart Plug schedules through routines in the Alexa app — for those two, set the on time yourself and adjust it as the evenings draw in, because no sunset tracking is recorded for them.

How much can one smart plug handle, and how many light strings is that?

Every plug here is rated 15A / 1800W, and that recorded limit — not a string count — is the honest answer. Each plug also controls a single outlet. Stay within the plug's 15A / 1800W limit and within the light string maker's own stated limit for the strings you connect, and read both figures off the products themselves: how many strings that allows depends on the strings you own, and this page invents no draw figures for them.

Will my holiday lights follow the schedule while I am away travelling?

Yes — that is what the away features are for. The Kasa EP25 and the Tapo P110M both record an Away Mode, which varies the on and off pattern so the house looks lived in rather than mechanically timed, and the Amazon Smart Plug's review records on and off control from the Alexa app when away from home. Set the schedule before you leave, and the lights keep the pattern while you travel, with no one home to switch them.

Does a smart plug for holiday lights need a hub, and will it work on 5GHz Wi-Fi?

No hub, for any of the three — each connects directly to Wi-Fi and is recorded as requiring no hub. None of them works on 5GHz: all three are 2.4GHz only, so the network has to offer a 2.4GHz band the plug can join. For the Tapo P110M, Matter pairing works best with the plug close to its controller, and for the Kasa EP25 the recorded setup struggle is band-steered mesh networks, where joining the 2.4GHz band during setup is the fix owners reach for.

Bottom Line

Buy the schedule, and let the off be automatic

Run the checks in order: a schedule that follows the dark or guarantees an off, a platform every voice in the house can reach, and a display inside the indoor and 15A / 1800W limits. Sunset-tracking for a HomeKit home: the Kasa EP25. Cross-platform, with Auto-Off as the backstop: the Tapo P110M. Simplest, in an all-Alexa house whose owners already run holiday lights this way: the Amazon Smart Plug, limits and all.
