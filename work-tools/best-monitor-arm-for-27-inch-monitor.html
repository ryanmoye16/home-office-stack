Buying Guide — Monitor Arms

Best Monitor Arm for a 27-Inch Monitor

The short answer first: a 27-inch monitor passes the size check on every arm we've reviewed — 13 to 32 inches on the HUANUO, 17 to 30 inches in the NB North Bayou F80's manual, up to 32 inches on the Amazon Basics — so size does not decide this purchase. What decides it is the panel you actually own: its weight without its factory stand, against ceilings of 22, 20 and 19.8 lb and, on the two gas springs, a 4.4 lb floor; how deeply it loads the tilt joint, a different question for a flat 27 than a curved one; and whether your desk gives a 27-inch screen the viewing distance it needs. Here are the three checks in order, then how the three arms split.

Check one: weigh the panel without its stand

Every monitor arm failure story starts with the wrong weight, and 27-inch panels invite the mistake. The figure printed on your monitor's box is the weight with the factory stand attached — and the stand comes off the moment the arm goes on. The figure an arm is rated against is the panel alone, and the only honest source for it is the manufacturer's specification page for your exact model. Do not guess from the diagonal: a 27-inch panel's weight without its stand varies by model, because panel depth, back material and built-in extras all change it while the screen size stays the same. Weigh the right number, then leave headroom under the ceiling — headroom absorbs a deep curved panel's tilt load and a heavier replacement screen later.

The three reviewed bands, at 27 inches. The HUANUO carries 4.4 to 22 lb, the highest ceiling of the three. The NB North Bayou F80 carries 4.4 to 19.8 lb, per its manual's 2.0 to 9.0 kg range. The Amazon Basics arm carries up to 20 lb with no spring floor, because there is no spring. Most 27-inch panels will land inside all three bands — which is why this check is about position inside the band, not pass or fail. A panel near the top of a band leaves that arm holding least comfortably; the same panel mid-band is the calmer install. If you'd rather not do this arithmetic by hand, our monitor arm checker takes your screen size, panel weight and VESA pattern and returns a strict-spec verdict on each of the three — comfortable fit, tight headroom, or outside spec — with no buying link on a fit that fails.

Gas springs add a second number: a floor. Both gas-spring arms we review are tensioned for panels from 4.4 lb upward. A very light 27-inch panel — the thinnest designs can be surprisingly light once the stand comes off — below that floor can refuse to stay lowered, floating up against a spring balanced for more weight. So the weight check is a band, not a ceiling: your panel needs to sit inside it. The standard arm has no floor at all, which makes an unusually light panel one of the honest cases for it.

Check two: how the panel loads the tilt joint at 27 inches

Weight is not the whole load — shape decides how that weight pulls. A monitor hangs off the arm's tilt joint, which holds the panel's angle against the leverage of its mass sitting out from the mount. A flat 27-inch panel is comparatively shallow: its mass sits close to the joint, and its weight reads much as the scale says. A curved 27 is built differently: the panel wraps, the body behind it is deeper, and that depth loads the tilt joint differently from a flat panel of the same stated weight. Our general home office monitor arm guide makes the same point across sizes — a panel at an arm's ceiling leaves nothing in reserve for this extra load — and at 27 inches the panel is wide enough that a tilt joint at its limit shows it plainly, as a slow downward drift owners re-tighten against.

So judge a curved 27 by position in the band, with extra room. If your panel is curved, or a deep-bodied flat design, prefer the arm where its weight sits furthest under the ceiling rather than the arm it barely fits. On the two gas springs, tension is the other half of this check: the spring is balanced for the panel it carries, and the F80's manual gives the adjustment a direction for each symptom — toward plus if the panel sinks, toward minus if it floats — so a tilt or height that will not hold is a setting to correct before it is a verdict on the arm. Owners of the HUANUO describe the same idea from the other side: stability once tensioned, per their reports. And if your real destination is wider than 27 inches later, that is a different problem — our ultrawide monitor arm guide runs the numbers on 34-inch panels, where size itself starts ruling arms out.

Check three: viewing distance and reach at 27 inches

A 27-inch screen needs more room than the 24-inch screen it usually replaces. Ergonomic guidance for seated computer work puts the screen roughly 20 to 40 inches from your eyes, with the top of the screen at or just below eye level. A 24-inch panel can sit happily toward the near end of that range; a 27 is wider and taller, so at the same distance it fills more of your view and its corners ask for more head movement. Most 27-inch setups settle in the middle of the range or beyond — so the honest question is not the arm's size rating but your desk's depth: measure from where your eyes sit to the desk's rear edge, and check a panel held at the back can reach a comfortable distance.

This is where the arm earns its place at this size. A factory stand parks a 27-inch panel wherever its base geometry puts it, and spends a large footprint of desk surface doing it. An arm mounts at the desk edge and returns that footprint as usable surface, while letting the panel sit further back than most stands allow — or pull forward for a call, and push back after. How much that movement matters is the mechanism question from our general guide: gas springs make the daily pull-and-push one-handed once tension is set, while the standard arm suits a screen positioned once at install. On a genuinely shallow desk the distance check can fail whichever arm you pick — a panel that cannot get far enough back is a desk problem first, and our monitor arm guide for small desks works through that case in full.

The tunable gas-spring pick: NB North Bayou F80

The F80 is the arm here with the long owner track record, and at 27 inches its case is tunability. The manual rates its spring at 2.0 to 9.0 kg — the same 4.4 to 19.8 lb — and gives the tension screw a direction for each symptom, toward plus for a panel that sinks and toward minus for one that floats, so the arm can be dialled to the exact panel it carries. That matters at this size because 27-inch panels spread widely inside the band: a light flat panel and a heavier curved one ask for visibly different tension. It adds one adjustment the other two do not list — pan, the fine sideways angle — alongside height, tilt, swivel and rotation, which makes it the easiest of the three to square a 27-inch screen to a desk that does not sit perfectly straight to the chair, and it carries an integrated cable channel.

Fit check: the F80 also carries the tightest band of the three — 4.4 to 19.8 lb, and 17 to 30 inches on its manual's figures — so the weight check matters most here: a 27-inch panel above 19.8 lb without its stand is outside the rating outright, however well the size fits. Assembly instructions are minimal, per owners, who put install at roughly 15 to 20 minutes with a helper for the final attachment. The included plastic cable clips feel flimsy to many owners — Velcro ties are the common upgrade. When none of these three is the right buy

A 27-inch panel above 22 lb without its stand sits outside everything we review, and needs an arm from the heavy-duty class, shopped on the same discipline: panel weight without the stand, headroom under the ceiling, VESA pattern confirmed. A VESA 200x100 panel rules out all three arms here regardless of weight — each accepts 75x75 and 100x100 only — and needs an arm specced for that pattern. The desk can rule all three out too: a clamp needs a solid rear edge it can bite, and a glass top or a thin hollow-core top is a poor candidate for a clamped load, while the grommet route needs a hole you have or are willing to drill. And two screens rule out all three as well: every arm we review is a single-monitor arm — a dual setup needs a dual arm or two singles, judged on the same checks per screen.

The other honest exit is a distance problem no arm solves. If your desk and room put your eyes closer than the 20 to 40 inch range to anywhere a 27-inch panel can sit — a shallow desk against a wall, a chair that cannot move back — a smaller panel or a deeper desk fixes what an arm cannot. Run your exact panel through the monitor arm checker before ordering any of the three; it applies these same specs strictly, and it will tell you when the honest answer is none of them.

27-inch monitor arm questions, answered honestly

Which monitor arm is best for a typical 27-inch monitor?

All three arms we review pass the size check at 27 inches, so the pick is your panel's weight and how often the screen moves. A mid-weight panel you reposition during the day suits either gas spring: the HUANUO, with the highest ceiling at 22 lb, or the NB North Bayou F80, rated 4.4 to 19.8 lb in its manual with adjustable tension and pan. A panel you set once and leave suits the Amazon Basics arm — straightforward install, steady hold on 24 to 27 inch panels per owners, stiffer adjustment. Weigh the panel without its stand first, then pick by movement.

How do I check whether my 27-inch panel fits an arm's weight rating?

Use the panel-only weight from your monitor manufacturer's specification page — the weight without its factory stand, since the arm replaces the stand. Do not use the shipping weight, which includes the stand and the box, and do not guess the weight from the diagonal, because 27-inch panels vary widely by model. Then judge the figure against the band, not just the ceiling: 4.4 to 22 lb on the HUANUO, 4.4 to 19.8 lb on the NB F80, and up to 20 lb on the Amazon Basics. Leave headroom rather than buying at the line.

Can a 27-inch panel be too light for a gas-spring arm?

Yes. Both gas springs we review are tensioned for panels from 4.4 lb upward, so a very light 27-inch panel below that floor may not stay lowered the way a mid-weight panel does. The F80's tension is adjustable — its manual turns the screw toward plus for a panel that sinks and toward minus for one that floats — which widens the usable band but does not remove the floor. A genuinely light panel is one honest case for the Amazon Basics arm instead: a standard arm has no spring and no minimum weight.

Does a curved 27-inch monitor need a stronger arm than a flat one?

It needs more headroom rather than a different class of arm. A flat panel's mass sits close to the tilt joint, while a deep-curved panel loads that joint differently, so the same panel weight asks more of the tilt hold on a curved screen. If your 27-inch panel is curved, judge its weight without the stand against the ceiling with extra room to spare, tension a gas spring for the panel it actually carries, and treat slow tilt drift after install as a sign the panel is too close to that arm's limit.

How far away should a 27-inch monitor sit, and does the arm change that?

Ergonomic guidance puts the screen roughly 20 to 40 inches from your eyes, with the top of the screen at or just below eye level. A 27-inch screen generally needs more of that range than the 24-inch it replaces, because the panel is wider and taller on the same desk. An arm helps on both ends: its mount sits at the desk edge, returning the factory stand's footprint as usable surface, and it can hold the panel further back on a deep desk or pull it closer for a call — as long as your desk's depth lets the panel reach a comfortable distance at all.

What if my 27-inch panel weighs over 22 lb without its stand, or has a VESA 200x100 pattern?

Then none of the three arms we review is the right buy, whatever the size check says. A panel over 22 lb without its stand sits above every ceiling here and needs a heavy-duty arm, shopped on the same discipline: panel weight without the stand, headroom under the ceiling, VESA pattern confirmed. A VESA 200x100 panel rules out all three regardless of weight — each accepts 75x75 and 100x100 only — and needs an arm specced for that pattern. Run your panel through our monitor arm checker before ordering; it will tell you when the honest answer is none of them.

Bottom Line

Size passes — weigh the panel, check the tilt, measure the distance

Run the checks in order: panel weight without its stand, inside the band with headroom; then the panel's shape at the tilt joint; then whether your desk gives a 27 its distance. A heavier or curved 27, or the most headroom you can get: the HUANUO, 22 lb ceiling on a gas spring. A mid-band 27 you want dialled in precisely, with pan on top: the NB North Bayou F80. One posture, an unusually light panel, or set it at install and forget it: the Amazon Basics arm. Above 22 lb, on VESA 200x100, or on a glass or hollow desk with no grommet option, buy nothing here — the checker above will point you at the right class instead.
