Buying Guide — Laptop Stands

Best Laptop Stand for Travel

The short answer first: travel is the harshest test a laptop stand can take, because a stand that works beautifully on one desk can fail completely in a bag. Two of the four stands we've reviewed fold flat. One only partially folds — its legs fold, but it never goes completely flat. And one does not fold at all, by design. Here are the three checks in order — does it actually fold flat, what does it add to what you carry, and does it still work when you arrive — then how the four split. For a fixed desk rather than a bag, start with our posture guide instead.

Check one: does it actually fold flat?

This check is binary, and it runs first because everything else is wasted on a stand that fails it. A travel stand has to go completely flat — flat enough to slide into a laptop bag alongside the laptop, and to set up again at the other end. Two fold flat: the Amazon Basics and the Lamicall Adjustable. One partially folds: the Lifelong Upryze, whose legs fold only — it does not go completely flat, and it needs storage space. And one does not fold at all: the Rain Design iLevel 2, a fixed ergonomic design built for a desk.

Partially folds is not a softer version of folds flat — it is a different job. A stand whose legs fold but whose frame stays three-dimensional can move between rooms, between homes, or in a car on a relocation. So read the Upryze here as a relocation stand, not a travel stand, and the iLevel 2 as no kind of portable at all — neither is a criticism of either stand at its own desk.

Folding flat also says nothing about folding easily. Owners describe the Amazon Basics folding and unfolding in seconds, with no tools and no loose parts. The Lamicall folds flat too, but its hinges are stiff by design: adjusting it takes two hands and real force, per owner reports and roundups. Both pass the fold check — with very different amounts of effort at each end of the trip.

Check two: what you carry — and what the catalog honestly does not say

Here is the honest finding first: only one of the four stands publishes its own weight in our catalog. The Lamicall states a stand weight of 2.1 lb. The Amazon Basics, the Lifelong Upryze and the Rain Design iLevel 2 state no stand weight in our catalog at all. We do not invent weights for the three, and you should not assume them either — an unstated weight cannot be checked, and that absence is itself part of this check. If a published carry weight decides your purchase, the Lamicall is the only one you can judge on it.

What can be judged for the others is folded bulk, qualitatively, exactly as catalogued. The Lamicall is bulkier folded than minimalist travel stands — its own review says so — because the large weighted base owners praise for its planted feel at a desk is also what travels with it. The Amazon Basics is catalogued as the drawer-and-bag stand, with no weight figure attached to that routine. The Upryze needs storage space, and the iLevel 2 keeps a fixed desk stand's full footprint.

So the carry decision is really about frequency. The Lamicall's catalog framing suits desk-first users and an occasional trip, where the destination desk earns the extra bulk. For a stand carried daily, only the Amazon Basics is described by owners in that bag routine — with its weight theirs to discover, not ours to state. Judge the known against the unknown honestly: an unstated weight is not a light one.

Check three: does it still work at the destination?

A stand that travels perfectly and lifts badly has only done half the job. The destination checks are height range and stated laptop-size ceiling, applied to a desk you do not control. On height: the Amazon Basics gives six preset levels from 3.5 to 6 inches; the Lamicall gives 5.7 to 8.1 inches with angle adjustable from 45° to 180°; the Upryze lifts up to 20 inches, far beyond a seated desk; and the iLevel 2 adjusts height and tilt by its front slider, a seated-desk mechanism. On stated size: up to 15.6 inches on the Amazon Basics, up to 17.3 inches on the Lamicall, 10 to 17 inches on the Upryze, and up to 17 inches on the iLevel 2. Our 17-inch laptop stand guide runs that size check in full.

Then the caveat that decides whether any of them works in practice: a raised laptop needs an external keyboard and mouse. Every stand lifts the built-in keyboard along with the screen, so typing on the raised machine puts your hands at stand height — and owners feel it: a bouncier feel at taller settings on the Lamicall, wobble typing at full height on the Upryze. Packing a stand without a keyboard is packing half a setup, so budget bag space for both.

When none of these four is the right travel buy

A laptop above 17.3 inches rules out every stand we review, folding or not — the Lamicall states the widest maximum, and rounding a larger machine down to a stated line is how stands fail quietly. A daily commute with a published, minimal carry weight as the deciding factor rules them out too: only the Lamicall publishes a stand weight at all, at 2.1 lb, and it is the bulkier folder of the two — so if a verified light weight is the purchase, shop beyond this catalog on the same three checks, and insist on a stated weight.

The other honest exit is a trip that is not really a trip. If you are relocating for weeks and want standing height at the far end, the Upryze's partial fold is a transport compromise worth making — in a car, with storage waiting. And if the stand will barely leave one desk, travel is the wrong frame entirely: our posture guide chooses among the same four by height.

Travel laptop stand questions, answered honestly

What makes a laptop stand a travel stand?

A travel stand folds flat, so it slides into a laptop bag and sets up again without tools or loose parts. Of the four stands we review, two pass that test: the Amazon Basics and the Lamicall Adjustable. The Lifelong Upryze only partially folds — its legs fold, but the stand does not go completely flat and needs storage space — and the Rain Design iLevel 2 does not fold at all. Folding flat is the entry ticket; what you carry and what works at the destination decide between the two that have it.

Is the fold-flat stand also the best desk stand?

Not automatically. Folding flat answers the bag question, not the desk question. At a fixed desk, the Rain Design iLevel 2 — the stand that never travels — adjusts height and tilt with its patented front slider while the laptop stays in place, which neither folder matches for in-day adjustment. The Lamicall is the folder that splits the difference: it folds flat, but its large weighted base and 5.7 to 8.1 inch height range are built desk-first.

Can I travel with a stand for a 17-inch laptop?

Yes, with one reviewed option. The Lamicall Adjustable folds flat and states a maximum of up to 17.3 inches, so it is the only reviewed stand that both travels and states headroom above 17 inches. It is also the only reviewed stand that publishes its own weight, at 2.1 lb, and it is bulkier folded than minimalist travel stands, with a large base. The Amazon Basics folds flat too, but stops at 15.6 inches, so a 17-inch laptop is outside its stated size.

Do I need an external keyboard and mouse when travelling with a stand?

For anything beyond a short session, yes. Every stand here lifts the laptop's built-in keyboard along with its screen, so typing on the raised machine puts your hands at stand height. Owners report the pattern stand by stand: a steady hold at normal typing speeds on the Amazon Basics, a bouncier feel at taller settings on the Lamicall, and wobble typing at full height on the Lifelong Upryze. An external keyboard and mouse keep typing movement off the stand — pack them as part of the travel setup.

What if my laptop is over 15.6 inches?

Then the travel pick changes, because the Amazon Basics states a maximum of 15.6 inches and a larger machine is outside it. The Lamicall Adjustable is the reviewed folder that still fits: it folds flat and states up to 17.3 inches. Above 17.3 inches, none of the four stands we review states a fit, folding or not — the Rain Design iLevel 2 stops at 17 inches and does not fold, and the Lifelong Upryze states 10 to 17 inches and only partially folds.

Bottom Line

Fold first, carry second, destination third

Run the checks in order: completely flat, or it is not a travel stand; then what it adds to your bag, on stated evidence rather than assumed weight; then height, size ceiling and an external keyboard at the destination. For most laptops up to 15.6 inches, take the Amazon Basics — the folder owners actually carry. For a laptop up to 17.3 inches, or a destination desk that matters more than bag weight, take the Lamicall Adjustable at its published 2.1 lb. The Lifelong Upryze is the relocation stand, and the Rain Design iLevel 2 is the fixed-desk stand — neither of them a bag stand.
