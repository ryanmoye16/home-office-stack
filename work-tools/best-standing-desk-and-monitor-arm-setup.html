Buying Guide — Standing Desks and Monitor Arms

Best Standing Desk and Monitor Arm Setup

A standing desk and a monitor arm are usually bought one at a time — and that is how a screen ends up wrong at one of the desk's two heights, or an arm and panel push a desktop past its stated limit. Bought as a setup, this guide runs three checks a single-product guide can skip. Travel: does the screen keep its position through the sit-stand swing, and what happens to the cables that cannot travel with it? Height at both ends: do the desk's presets and the arm's adjustment divide the work so the screen is right sitting and standing, not just one of the two? And honest counting: do the arm and panel fit inside the desk's desktop limit, and does the clamp land on a solid piece of top? One desk's limit is tight enough that the counting, not the pairing, decides the purchase.

Check one: the arm travels with the desk — the cables to the wall do not

Mount the arm to the desktop and the screen stops being a separate object every time the desk moves. Every arm we review fits by C-clamp or grommet through the top, so raise the surface and the screen rises with it, keeping the position the arm set relative to the desktop. A factory stand travels too, but at the height it was built at; an arm travels with a height you chose.

What does not travel is anything anchored off the desk. Any cable running from the moving desktop to a fixed point crosses a span that changes length every time the desk changes height — by the full distance between the desk's lowest and highest settings, a travel these four desks measure between roughly 17 and 26 inches. A cable tidy at sitting height and anchored at both ends is a cable being pulled at standing height, at its ports, every day.

The fix is a single deliberate slack run, sized for full travel. Everything on the desk plugs in on the desk, and one run crosses to the wall or floor with enough slack that it is still slack at the desk's highest setting — not straight, not lifting, not holding any weight. Judge the run at the top of the travel, never at the bottom.

Check two: screen height at both saved heights — presets do the big move, the arm does the fine tune

A standing desk is used at two heights, so a screen height checked at one has only been half checked. The common failure is subtle: the screen is set perfectly at sitting height, the desk rises, and the screen — carried up by exactly the desk's travel — now sits at a height dictated by the desk's range rather than by your eyes, and can need a small adjustment standing. A pairing works when the two mechanisms divide that labour.

The desk's presets do the big move, and all four desks here carry them. The SHW, the FLEXISPOT EN1 and the Marsail each carry four memory presets; the VIVO carries three, alongside a timer. Saving both is what makes the arm's half of the job small: the preset carries the desktop, the arm and the screen between the two positions in one press.

The arm does the fine tune, and the arms split on how willingly they do it. The HUANUO and the NB North Bayou F80 are gas-spring arms: they re-tune with a light push, so correcting the screen by the small amount a standing position asks for is a one-hand, few-seconds job at each end — the right character for a setup whose screen height genuinely differs between sitting and standing. The Amazon Basics arm is the opposite character: stiff and set-and-forget, so each fine tune is a deliberate effort. If your two positions share one screen height — the preset move alone lands the screen right at both ends — the stiff arm is set once and never asked again. If they do not, a gas-spring arm earns its place every single day.

Check three: load and clamp, counted honestly — the arm is part of the desk's budget

The arm and its panel are weight on the desktop, and they count toward the desk's limit. The arm sits on the top, the panel hangs from the arm, and both are carried by the desk's motors at every move, alongside everything else on the surface. Counted honestly, the arm-plus-panel total comes off the desk's stated limit before anything else is added — and on one desk here, that counting is the decision.

The gates differ more than the desks' shapes suggest. The SHW's manual states a 110 lb limit — the tight gate of the four, on the desk that also includes a drawer and a cable tray in the box. The FLEXISPOT EN1, the VIVO and the Marsail each allow 176 lb on the desktop. The VIVO also carries a 220 lb figure — frame support only, not a desktop load, and never a target to load toward. The arm side of the sum is bounded by each arm's own ceiling — up to 22 lb of panel on the HUANUO, the highest of the three, up to 19.8 lb on the F80 across its manual's 17 to 30 inch range, and up to 20 lb on the Amazon Basics arm, all three on VESA 75x75 or 100x100 — but the panel passing the arm's check does not finish the sum: the arm and panel then go on the desk's side of the ledger, with headroom left under its number.

The clamp spot is the second half of honest counting, because a clamp loads one small patch of top, not the whole surface. The VIVO's 63.1 by 31.5 inch top is a 3-piece top, and some owners can feel its seams — a clamp centred on a seam grips the joint between pieces. The FLEXISPOT EN1 removes that question at the most common clamp position: its top is one piece, with no centre seam to flex under a centre clamp. Run a hand along the edge, choose a spot that is one solid piece of top, and re-check the clamp's grip after the first days of full-height travel.

When the desk or arm already in your room is the right answer

A desk and an arm bought separately are not automatically mismatched: if your screen travels with the desk and holds its height at both saved positions, the one slack run already survives full travel without pulling, and the arm and panel already sit visibly inside the desk's desktop limit on a clamp that grips a solid stretch of top, no pairing here buys you anything new. The cases that earn a rethink are specific — a screen right at one height and wrong at the other, a cable that tugs near full extension, a total that only fits if the arm is pretended weightless, a clamp centred on a seam. Change only the failing half.

Before you buy: the five-minute setup test

First, the travel test: trace every cable that would run from the desktop to the wall or floor, and confirm one slack run can cover the desk's full travel, still slack at the top. Second, the two-height test: confirm the desk carries at least two saveable presets — all four here do, three on the VIVO and four on the others — and decide honestly whether your screen height is the same at both ends or differs, because that answer chooses gas spring or set-once. Third, the ledger test: add the arm and the panel, without its stand, to everything else on the top, and place the total against the desk's desktop limit — 110 lb on the SHW, 176 lb on the EN1, the VIVO and the Marsail, with the VIVO's 220 lb frame figure left out of the sum entirely. Fourth, the clamp test: run a hand along the back edge, find the seams if your top has them, and name the solid spot the clamp will grip. Those answers usually name the pairing.

Standing desk and monitor arm setup questions, answered honestly

What is the best standing desk and monitor arm setup?

Of the products we review, it depends on the desk's headroom and on how you re-tune the screen. Every desk here carries memory presets — four on the SHW, the FLEXISPOT EN1 and the Marsail, three on the VIVO — so the desk does the big move and the arm only fine-tunes. The gas-spring HUANUO and NB North Bayou F80 arms re-tune with a light push. The Amazon Basics arm is stiff and set-and-forget, which suits a user whose two positions share one screen height. Either way, the arm and panel count toward the desk's limit: 110 lb on the SHW, 176 lb on the EN1, the VIVO and the Marsail.

Does a monitor arm move with a standing desk?

Yes, when the arm is mounted to the desktop itself. A monitor arm clamped or grommet-mounted to the top travels with the desk through the whole sit-stand swing, keeping the position it set at every height. What does not travel is anything anchored off the desk: cables to a wall outlet, a floor-standing computer, or the floor itself. Those runs cross the moving span, so they need one slack run sized for the desk's full travel, never pulled taut at full standing height.

How do I set the screen height for both sitting and standing?

The desk's memory presets do the big move: save your sitting and standing elbow heights, and the preset carries the desktop — and the arm and screen mounted to it — between the two. The arm then does the fine tune at each end, because eye level does not rise by exactly the same amount as the desktop for every user. Gas-spring arms such as the HUANUO and the F80 make that tune a light push; the stiff Amazon Basics arm suits a set-once user whose positions share one screen height. Check the screen at both saved heights, not just one.

Does a monitor arm count toward a standing desk's weight limit?

Yes. The arm and the panel it carries sit on the desktop, so their weight counts toward the desk's desktop limit, alongside everything else on the surface. The gates differ a lot: the SHW's manual limit is 110 lb, the tight gate of the four, while the EN1, the VIVO and the Marsail each allow 176 lb on the desktop. The VIVO also carries a 220 lb figure, but that is frame support only — never load the desktop to it.

Where should a monitor arm clamp on a standing desk top?

On a solid stretch of top, away from a seam where you can choose. The clamp concentrates the arm's load at one spot on the edge, so what is under that spot matters. The VIVO 63 by 32 top is a 3-piece top, and some owners can feel its seams; clamping directly on a seam puts the arm's grip on the joint between pieces rather than on one solid piece. The FLEXISPOT EN1 avoids the question at the centre: its 48 by 24 inch top is one piece, with no centre seam to flex under a centre clamp. Run your hand along the edge first, pick one solid piece of top, and re-check the clamp after the first full-height runs.

Bottom Line

Let the desk do the big move, the arm do the fine tune, and count both halves honestly

Run the checks in order: arm mounted to the top, one slack run sized for full travel, both heights saved to the presets with the arm fine-tuning at each end, and the arm plus panel counted against the desk's desktop limit with the clamp on a solid piece of top. The fine-tune answer: a gas-spring arm — the HUANUO Single Monitor Arm for the highest panel capacity here, the NB North Bayou F80 for pan and its integrated cable channel. The set-once answer: the Amazon Basics Single Monitor Arm, stiff by design, on any desk whose preset move lands the screen right at both heights. The desk answers: the FLEXISPOT EN1 for a centre clamp on a one-piece top, the VIVO 63 x 32 for the widest range with its seams and load figures read honestly, the Marsail for the same headline numbers with no extras, and the SHW 48-Inch only when the whole setup totals visibly under its 110 lb manual limit.
