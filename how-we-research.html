Our Method

How We Research

Every review and guide on Home Office Stack is research-based. That is a deliberate choice, and this page is the rule sheet for it: where our facts come from, what we refuse to invent, and how a page gets corrected when the record changes. If a review ever disagrees with this page, this page wins.

Research-based means exactly that

We do not claim hands-on testing we have not done. You will not find "in our tests" or "after two weeks on our desk" in a Home Office Stack review, because that is not how the review was made. What you will find is the work that actually happened: the manufacturer's own specifications checked line by line, and published feedback from people who own and use the product every day — long-term owner reviews, owner discussions, and reputable roundups — read for the patterns that only show up after weeks of daily use.

We think that trade is honest, and often more useful than a single reviewer's first impression: one person's desk for a day tells you less than hundreds of owners reporting the same stiff hinge, the same flimsy cable clip, or the same arm still holding its position a year later.

Where the facts come from

Specs come from the maker. Dimensions, weight capacities, port layouts, and compatibility limits are taken from the manufacturer's product page, manual, or published spec sheet — not copied from a retail listing's summary, which is where errors breed. When two manufacturer sources disagree, we publish the conservative figure and note the conflict rather than picking the number that makes a better headline.

Experience comes from owners. The praise and the complaints in a review are the recurring themes in published owner feedback: what breaks, what annoys, what quietly works. A single dramatic review — glowing or furious — moves nothing; a pattern across many owners does. For software tools, we verify features and pricing against each platform's own published terms and weigh published user and creator feedback the same way.

Fit comes before verdicts. Every review is organized around who a product is actually for: the desk size, monitor weight, laptop, or workflow it has to fit. A product that fails its fit check gets a "skip if" in plain language, however good its specs look — our ergonomic desk setup guide shows the criteria-first approach applied across a whole desk.

Numbers we show — and numbers we never invent

No displayed prices. Prices on Amazon change too often for a static page to state one honestly, so our buttons say "Check price on Amazon" and the listing you land on is the price that counts.

Ratings are dated. Where a review shows a star rating or review count, it is a figure observed on the product's Amazon listing, and the page says when — look for the "as of" date next to it. Those numbers age, and we would rather show their age than pretend they are live.

Every product link is sourced. A product only gets an Amazon link when its listing has been verified from a recorded source. If a fact — a rating, a specification, a listing — cannot be verified, it is left out of the page, not estimated, rounded into existence, or borrowed from a similar product.

What we will not do

✓

No sponsored reviews or paid placement

No brand can pay to be reviewed, ranked, or recommended. Every product gets the same research treatment whether a brand contacts us or not.

✓

No fabricated experience

No invented testing, no fake testimonials, no "readers say" quotes. Owner feedback is reported as owner feedback, in aggregate, with its limits attached.

✓

No silent superlatives

Claims like "best-selling" or "top-rated" need a verifiable source or they come out. Specs and fit checks carry our recommendations, not marketing labels.

✓

Honest limits, in the review

Every review carries what could be better alongside what we liked, and every guide has a "when none of these is right" route. A recommendation you cannot talk yourself out of is an ad, not a review.

Corrections and updates

Research-based publishing only works if errors get fixed at the source. Our catalog data lives in one structured file that generates the review pages and grids, so when a manufacturer's page or manual contradicts what we published — it has happened, on something as basic as how many HDMI ports a hub has — the correction is made once, at the source, and re-rendered everywhere the claim appeared. Pages carry publication and update dates in their structured data, ratings carry their "as of" date in the text, and a fix is never quietly swapped in as if the error had not existed.

Spotted something we got wrong? Tell us at support [at] homeofficestack.com — corrections from readers get the same source-check as everything else here, and verified ones get fixed.
