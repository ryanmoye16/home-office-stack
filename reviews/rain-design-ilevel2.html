What We Liked

The front slider is the feature owners and reviewers single out. Sliding the knob on the front of the stand raises or lowers the platform — and tilts it — with the laptop still on it, where most stands make you lift the laptop off, adjust, and put it back. Owners describe resetting height for a video call or a posture change as a seconds-long job.

The anodized aluminum platform is the other recurring positive. Owner feedback describes a sturdy hold once the height is set, with rubber pads that grip the desk and protect the laptop's underside.

The open design does two jobs owners appreciate: the aluminum helps conduct heat away from the laptop instead of trapping it underneath, and the open back gives cables somewhere to hide instead of spilling across the desk. The silver and space gray finishes also match Apple hardware closely, which MacBook owners mention often.

What Could Be Better

It does not fold — this is a fixed, desk-only stand. If you work from more than one place or travel with your stand, a fold-flat design fits that life better.

Its height range is a seated-desk range. It raises a laptop toward eye level for sitting, but it does not reach standing height — shoppers who want to stand need a tall riser instead.

There is no VESA mount, so it will not integrate with a monitor-arm setup.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback centers on the slider in daily use: it moves smoothly, stays where it is set without slippage, and makes height changes quick enough that owners actually make them — switching between a typing position and a higher video-call position in the same day.

The open aluminum design is the other consistent report: air keeps moving under the laptop instead of heat building up against a closed platform, which matters most on machines that vent through the underside during long sessions.

Who This Is For

MacBook and ultrabook users who want a desk-bound aluminum stand they can adjust without lifting the laptop off it. If your stand will live on one seated desk and you change height during the day — for calls, posture shifts, or a shared desk — the front slider is this stand's reason to exist. Travelers and standing-desk users should look at fold-flat or tall stands instead. Shopping for posture first? Our laptop stand guide for posture compares all four stands we review by height range.

Bottom Line

The Rain Design iLevel 2 is the fixed-desk laptop stand built around one idea: height and tilt you can change with the laptop still on the stand, in a solid aluminum frame that conducts heat and hides cables at the back. Its limits are the flip side of that focus — no folding, no standing height, no VESA mount.
