What We Liked

The controls are the ones people actually use. The LED handset shows the current height in inches and stores four memory presets, so sitting and standing heights are one press each — and the manual's own posture bands (sitting at 28 to 32 inches, standing at 38 to 45) sit inside the desk's range, which means the presets cover the full sit-stand swing for the users the range fits. Four presets is also one more than several listings in this class offer, which matters in a shared or two-posture-plus-perch household.

The box contents finish the desk. A storage drawer with adjustable compartments, an under-desk cable tray, a desktop grommet, cable clamps and two utility hooks are all included and all listed in the manual's package contents — on many budget desks the tray and drawer are the second and third purchases. For a first standing desk, arriving complete is a real part of the value owners keep naming.

The footprint is the right size for the rooms these desks actually go into. At 48 by 24 inches it takes a laptop plus a monitor, or two modest monitors on a shared stand, without eating a bedroom wall the way a 55 or 60 inch top does — and the Walnut finish on this listing reads as furniture rather than office equipment, which owners in small multi-use rooms mention approvingly.

Assembly feedback is consistently kind for flat-pack furniture. The manual is pictorial and step-ordered, the hardware set includes the Allen wrenches, and owner reports on SHW's electric desks put the build at roughly half an hour to an hour; the manual recommends two people, mainly for the lift-and-flip moments with the top. The leveling glides then take out the wobble that an uneven floor would otherwise hand you.

What Could Be Better

The 110 lb weight limit is this desk's defining ceiling, and it is lower than it sounds once you count honestly. That figure is everything on the desktop — monitors, an arm if you clamp one on, a laptop, a desktop PC if it lives on top, books and the rest. A light laptop-and-monitor setup clears it comfortably; a tower PC plus dual monitors on a heavy arm can approach it. The manual is blunt about why it matters: overload can damage the motor and frame, and the desk's overload protection stops movement mid-travel. Count your load before you buy, and keep headroom under the limit rather than landing on it.

The 28 inch minimum height is high for shorter seated users. Standard desk height is not a fit guarantee: if your seated elbow height lands below 28 inches, the desktop sits above your elbows and your shoulders shrug to meet it. The honest fixes are a footrest to raise the chair, or a keyboard tray — this model has neither in the box, and its own drawer occupies the under-desk centre where a tray would go. Petite users should treat the minimum height as the first fit check, not a footnote.

The 45 inch maximum has the mirror-image limit at the other end. The manual's standing band tops out at 45 inches, which covers most users; a tall standing user whose elbow height needs more than that is outside this desk's range, and no accessory adds lift range. Likewise, owners of SHW's wider electric desks report a slight wobble at maximum height — a behaviour common to two-leg electric frames at full extension, and one to expect here at 45 inches rather than be surprised by, especially typing hard on an uneven floor before the glides are dialled in.

The top is engineered wood with a laminated finish, not solid wood, and the warranty story is thin on paper: the manual defers to the warranty card in the box rather than publishing a term, so do not buy this desk on the strength of a warranty length nobody states. A minority of owners across SHW's listings report motor or support problems; they are outnumbered heavily in a 21,700+ rating pool averaging 4.4 stars, but they are the reason this review scores the desk on its fit and its box contents, not on a durability promise.

Performance in Daily Use

Day to day, the desk's performance is the motor's behaviour, and the manual tells you more about it than the marketing does. Movement is press-and-hold on the handset or one press to a stored preset; if the desk stops mid-travel, the manual's first suspect is overload, and its fix is to reduce the load and let the motor cool for 20 to 30 minutes after continuous use — thermal protection is a real feature of motors in this class, and a desk that pauses after repeated full-range cycles in a hot room is behaving as documented, not failing. Uneven movement or unusual noise points, per the same troubleshooting table, to an uneven load or a loose fastener before it points to a defect: distribute the weight, re-tighten, and check for obstructions in the travel path.

Owner feedback on SHW's electric desks lines up with that picture. The recurring positives are a smooth, quiet-enough lift for shared rooms, a frame that feels sturdier than buyers expected at this level, and motors still cycling daily years in — the installed base behind the 48 inch listing is one of the largest in the budget electric category, which is itself a durability signal no lab test substitutes for. The recurring negatives are the ones already named above: movement quirks that trace to load and levelling, and a support experience a minority found slow when something did go wrong.

Stability follows the same pattern as every two-leg electric desk we cover: planted at sitting heights, and at standing heights it depends on the glides being levelled and the load being centred. The included cable tray earns its keep here in a way owners underline — a sit-stand desk drags every cable through its full travel twice a day, and routing them through the tray and grommet is what stops a monitor lead becoming the thing that limits the lift.

Who This Is For

Remote workers with a light-to-medium setup — a laptop and one monitor, or two modest monitors on their own stands — who want a first electric sit-stand desk that arrives complete: presets, drawer, cable tray and hooks in one box, in a 48 inch footprint that fits a bedroom or a small study, in a finish that does not look like office surplus. It is especially relevant if your current fixed desk is the wrong height in both directions and the four presets would end the daily compromise.

Skip it if you are a shorter seated user whose elbows land well below a 28 inch desktop and you do not want a footrest in the setup. And skip it if you are tall enough standing to need more than 45 inches at the elbow: the range is the range.

Will It Fit You and Your Setup? The Height, Load and Surface Check

A standing desk carries three fit facts, and they matter more than any feature on it: the height range is 28 to 45 inches, the weight limit is 110 lb, and the surface is 48 by 24 inches. Everything else follows from those three, so check them against your own body and your own equipment list — your seated and standing elbow heights, everything that will sit on the desktop, and the screens you run — before anything else on this page matters.

Height is the first check because the manual publishes its own posture bands: sitting at 28 to 32 inches and standing at 38 to 45. If your seated elbow height falls below 28, the desk cannot come down to you — the fix is raising yourself (a taller chair setting plus a footrest), not a setting on the desk. At the standing end, 45 inches is the ceiling; if your standing elbow height needs more, this frame is simply too short, and the honest move is a desk with a taller range. The load check is arithmetic, not vibes: weigh or look up everything that lives on the top, add it, and keep the total comfortably under 110 lb — the headroom rule we use for monitor arms (stay under about three quarters of the rating) is a sane way to leave margin for the books and hardware that accumulate.

What the Included Extras Actually Replace — and What They Do Not

Budget electric desks are usually priced as a frame and a top, and the ownership costs arrive afterwards: a cable tray, a drawer unit, hooks, clamps. This listing's package contents, per its own manual, include all of them — the drawer with adjustable compartments, the under-desk cable tray, four cable clamps, a desktop grommet and two utility hooks — so the comparison that matters is not this headline against a bare frame's headline, but this box against a frame plus the second order you would otherwise place in the first month.

What they do not replace is worth stating with the same clarity. The drawer is a shallow organiser for stationery and small items, not file storage. The cable tray manages the desk's own moving cables — the critical job on a sit-stand desk, since every lead travels the full 17 inches of lift — but it is not a surge-protected power solution, and a power strip still needs a home, ideally in the tray itself. And none of the extras changes the two numbers that decide fit: 28 to 45 inches of range and 110 lb of capacity. Extras are the tiebreaker between desks that fit you, never a substitute for one that does.
