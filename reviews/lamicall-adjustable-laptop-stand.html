What We Liked

Height and angle both adjust, so owners can dial the screen toward eye level instead of accepting one fixed riser height. The larger, heavier aluminum base is the detail roundups and owners single out: it keeps even large laptops planted with less shaking while typing than lighter folding stands. Ventilation holes in the tray improve airflow under the laptop, anti-slip silicone pads and front hooks keep it from sliding or scratching, and the stand folds flat enough to slip into a laptop bag for travel or hot-desking.

What Could Be Better

At 2.1 lbs it is heavier and bulkier folded than minimalist travel stands, and the large base needs real desk footprint. The hinges are stiff by design — adjusting takes two hands and real force, and roundups note hinges can need tightening over time. Typing directly on the raised laptop can feel bouncy, especially at taller settings with heavier machines, so it works best with an external keyboard and mouse. It is a seated-desk riser, not a standing-height stand.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback and roundup testing converge on the same picture: the weighted base stays planted during everyday typing, the angle holds once set because the hinges are tight, and the open ventilated tray leaves the laptop underside exposed to air. The consistent caveats are effort — height changes are a two-handed job — and stability at the top of the range, where heavier laptops can feel less planted and typing on the built-in keyboard gets bouncy. None of that requires a standing setup: the 5.7 to 8.1 inch lift is a seated posture fix, best paired with a separate keyboard at elbow height.

Who This Is For

Remote and hybrid workers who want one adjustable stand for a home desk that can also fold flat for occasional travel, and anyone with a 10 to 17.3 inch laptop whose current setup leaves the screen well below eye level. It suits desk-first users more than everyday commuters — if you carry your stand daily, a lighter minimalist folder will feel better in the bag. If you need standing height, look at a tall riser instead. Shopping for posture first? Our laptop stand guide for posture compares all four stands we review by height range.

Bottom Line

The Lamicall earns its place as the general-purpose pick in this catalog: real height and angle adjustment, a base heavy enough to stay planted, and fold-flat portability in one aluminum stand. Buy it for a desk that sometimes travels. Skip it if you need standing height, want the lightest possible travel stand, or plan to type on the laptop itself at full height.
