What We Liked

Setup is what owners praise most, and it is genuinely the simplest in the category: Amazon's Wi-Fi Simple Setup connects the plug to your network in a few steps through the Alexa app, there is no hub to install, and there is no separate vendor app or account to manage — control, naming, and grouping all live in the Alexa app alongside your Echo devices.

Once it is running, owners use it for the unglamorous jobs a smart plug is actually for: Alexa routines and schedules for bedside and floor lamps, fans, holiday lights, and coffee makers or other devices with a physical on/off switch, plus on/off control from the Alexa app when away from home. The 15A rating covers typical household loads, and the single-socket body is a tidy one-outlet unit rather than a power strip.

What Could Be Better

Alexa only — and that is Amazon's own wording, not a missing feature waiting on an update: the plug is built for exclusive use with Alexa and does not support other voice assistants or smart hubs, so Google Home and Apple Home households should look elsewhere from the start.

There is no energy monitoring — the plug reports nothing about power draw, so standby-hunting and usage tracking need a monitoring plug instead. It is also 2.4GHz Wi-Fi only, with no 5GHz support: owner complaints concentrate exactly there, on homes where the 2.4GHz band is weak, hard to select, or band-steered, and a weak signal at the outlet shows up as sporadic unresponsiveness. Indoor use only, and each plug controls a single outlet.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback splits the same way it does for most Wi-Fi plugs: the experience depends on the 2.4GHz network underneath it. Once configured, owners consistently describe day-to-day operation as stable and boring in the best sense — voice commands and routines fire as expected for lamps, fans, and coffee makers, schedules run on time, and the plug reconnects after power or Wi-Fi outages. The recurring complaints are not about daily control but about setup on difficult networks and about the two hard limits above: no other voice platform, and no energy data.

Who This Is For

Alexa households that want the simplest possible on/off control for a lamp, fan, coffee maker, or similar device with a physical on/off switch, without a hub or another app. If you need energy monitoring, the TP-Link Kasa EP25 adds it for HomeKit homes and the Tapo P110M adds it on Matter — and either is also the better pick if your home is not all-in on Alexa.

Bottom Line

The Amazon Smart Plug is the right buy when the brief is narrow: an Alexa-only home that wants simple, hub-free on/off control and scheduling for ordinary lamps and appliances, set up once in the Alexa app and forgotten. Judged on that brief it is a clean, well-supported plug with a 1-year limited warranty. Judged on anything broader — other voice platforms, energy monitoring, 5GHz networks — it is the wrong plug by design, and the EP25 or P110M fit better.
