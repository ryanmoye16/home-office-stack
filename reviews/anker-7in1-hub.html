What We Liked

The port selection is the real story here. Two USB-A ports for legacy devices, a USB-C data port, HDMI for an external display, and both SD and microSD slots — which owners can use at the same time, a detail photographers single out when copying a camera card and a drone card in one session. Having all of that in a device smaller than a deck of cards is the appeal.

Charging is honestly rated once you read Anker's own FAQ: the PD port takes up to 100W in, reserves 15W for the hub, and delivers up to 85W to the laptop. That is full speed for a MacBook Air and most ultrabooks while every other port stays active — as long as the charger feeding it is strong enough (Anker's guidance and owner reports agree a weak adapter is the first suspect when a display flickers).

Owner feedback on stability is consistent: roundup summaries of owner reviews report connections holding under load, with no disconnects or transfer failures even on large transfers, and reviewers describe the build as compact and sturdy enough to live in a laptop bag.

The HDMI output comes with an honest ceiling, covered below: 4K runs at 30Hz, which owners find fine for documents, presentations, and media, while 1080p and 1440p run at a smooth 60Hz.

What Could Be Better

No Gigabit Ethernet port. If you need wired internet (common in hotel rooms or offices with Wi-Fi restrictions), look at Anker's own 555 hub instead, which builds Ethernet in.

The HDMI port caps 4K at 30Hz. That is usable for static work but noticeably less smooth for heavy window management on a 4K monitor; if 4K@60Hz matters, the Anker 555 is the step up. And video only ever comes out of the HDMI port — Anker states the USB-C ports carry no display signal, even through a USB-C-to-HDMI cable.

The attached cable is short, the most common owner complaint: it keeps the hub close to the laptop, which limits placement and can strain the port on some desk setups. There is also no 3.5mm audio jack, so wired headphones need the laptop's own jack or a separate adapter.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback on the 7-in-1 (Anker's model 341) is consistent across long-term reviews and roundup summaries: connections stay stable with a display, peripherals, and card imports running together; both card slots read at the same time, at UHS-I speeds — dependable for casual photo and clip offloads, not the tool for dumping a huge shoot in a hurry, where a dedicated UHS-II reader is faster.

Heat comes up as moderate rather than a problem. Reviewers report the body staying manageable under extended use with a display and drives attached, and Anker itself says warmth during charging or fast transfers is normal — just don't cover the hub under load.

The one recurring caution is charger sensitivity: pass-through power depends on the adapter you feed it. A 100W PD charger is what delivers the full 85W to the laptop, roundups recommend 60W or higher for laptops generally, and low-wattage adapters are the reported cause when an external display flickers.

Who This Is For

If you have a USB-C laptop (MacBook, Dell XPS, Lenovo ThinkPad, Samsung Galaxy Book) and you want one small hub that covers almost everything on the road, this is the one to get. It's especially good for:

Remote workers — the port variety means you can connect to almost any office or hotel setup, as long as you don't need wired Ethernet.

Photographers and creators — SD and microSD slots that work simultaneously make card transfers trivial.

MacBook Air and ultrabook users — 85W pass-through covers their charging needs without compromise.

Skip it if you need 4K at 60Hz, Ethernet, or 10Gbps drive speeds — those are exactly what the larger Anker 555 adds.

Bottom Line

The Anker 7-in-1 USB-C Hub (341) earns its place as the travel hub in this lineup: the right seven ports, both card slots, honest 85W pass-through charging, and a body small enough to forget in your bag. Its limits are equally clear — 4K stops at 30Hz, there is no Ethernet, and data runs at 5Gbps. If those limits don't touch your work, this is the hub to carry; if they do, step up to the 555.
