What We Liked

The height range is the standout, and it is not close. Owner feedback and roundups consistently single out the up-to-20-inch lift: it can raise a laptop screen toward standing eye level without a separate monitor, something typical stands that stop around 6 to 8 inches cannot do.

Owners describe the aluminum and steel frame as sturdy, with laptops held securely once the adjustment knob is tightened. Verified owner reviews at Target call it sturdy and highly adjustable, easy to move between heights, and still like new after two months of use.

The open platform design leaves the laptop's underside exposed to airflow instead of trapping it against a solid shelf.

What Could Be Better

Height this tall comes with a trade-off owners and roundups both note: at or near maximum extension the stand can wobble more if you type on the laptop itself, so an external keyboard and mouse are practically mandatory at standing height.

Height changes mean loosening a knob and repositioning the stand — slower than a slider stand, and repositioning with the laptop in place takes some effort; that stiffness is part of how it holds its set height.

The partial-fold design means it does not go completely flat, so it needs storage space, and the functional grey finish is a tool look rather than a desk ornament.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback centers on the standing use case: with the laptop raised toward standing height and a separate keyboard and mouse on the desk, the Upryze lets the laptop screen stand in for a monitor. Roundups that rank it for extra height make the same pairing point — at full reach, typing on the raised laptop itself amplifies movement, while a separate keyboard keeps the setup steady.

Adjustment is deliberate rather than instant: loosening the knob, repositioning, and re-tightening is slower than the Rain Design iLevel 2's front slider, but the range it covers is substantially greater.

Who This Is For

Remote workers who want to use their laptop at standing height without buying a separate monitor, and taller users whose seated eye level sits above what a standard riser reaches. It also suits shared or hot-desking setups where the stand gets reset to different users' heights. It assumes you already have an external keyboard and mouse — at tall settings they are not optional. Shopping for posture first? Our laptop stand guide for posture compares all four stands we review by height range.

Bottom Line

The Lifelong Upryze is the tall stand in this catalog: up to 20 inches of lift for 10 to 17 inch laptops, an aluminum and steel frame owners describe as sturdy, and honest limits — knob adjustment that takes effort and more movement at full height if you type on the laptop itself. Buy it when you genuinely need standing height; for a seated desk, a smaller stand does the job with less fuss.
