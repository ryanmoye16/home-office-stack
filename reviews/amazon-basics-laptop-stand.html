Why a Laptop Stand Matters

If you work from home with a laptop, your neck is probably paying the price. Every inch you tilt your head forward multiplies the strain on your cervical spine. A laptop stand raises the screen to eye level, letting you use an external keyboard and mouse — the correct ergonomic setup.


Beyond ergonomics, laptop stands also:

  • Keep your laptop cooler with ventilated airflow underneath
  • Clear desk space by raising the laptop off the surface
  • Make external monitor setups easier to position
  • Fold flat for working from cafes or traveling

Setup and Use

No assembly required. You unfold the stand, place your laptop on the rubberized pads, and that's it. The 6 height positions click into place firmly — no wobbling once set. Switching between positions takes about 3 seconds.


The fold-flat design is genuinely useful. I travel with this stand regularly and it slides into my laptop bag without adding meaningful bulk. Most portable stands compromise on stability; this one doesn't.

Build Quality

For a $20 stand, the build quality is surprisingly solid. The aluminum construction feels sturdy — no flex when you press down on the keyboard area. The rubber pads on the laptop rest and bottom feet keep everything in place and prevent scratches.


The one limitation: this stand is designed for laptops 15.6" and under. If you have a 16" MacBook Pro or a larger gaming laptop, the stand still works but the laptop will overhang the edges. That's fine functionally, but it looks a bit awkward.

What Could Be Better

The fixed angle is the main trade-off versus pricier stands. If you want to type at a steeper angle (like an inclined reading stand), you'll need a different model. Some competitors at $40-60 offer a tilting or rotating platform, which is useful if you switch between typing and reading frequently.


Also: there's no dedicated channel for cable management. If you're routing a USB-C cable from your laptop to a hub below, it'll just drape over the side. Minor, but noticeable.

Who It's For

Anyone who uses a laptop at a desk and doesn't want to spend more than $25 on a stand. If you already have a premium desk setup with a monitor arm and mechanical keyboard, you're probably past this stand. But for the vast majority of work-from-home setups, this is exactly the right level of support without overthinking it.
