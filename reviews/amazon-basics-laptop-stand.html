What We Liked

Six height levels cover the useful range for most seated desks, and owner feedback is consistent that the aluminum frame holds its set position without wobble or flex at normal typing speeds. Silicone pads and front hooks keep the laptop seated and unscratched.

The open design keeps air moving under the laptop — owners credit it with cooler-running machines — and the space underneath stays usable for a mouse or notebook. It folds flat, so the same stand moves between a home desk, a drawer, and a laptop bag without any parts to lose.

What Could Be Better

Height comes in six preset steps, not fine adjustment, so your ideal eye-level height can fall between two settings — and changing levels takes two hands, per owner reports. The stand also feels least planted at its highest setting; typing stability is best on a flat, solid surface.

The fit ceiling is explicit: laptops up to 15.6 inches and 10 lbs, which rules out heavy 16-inch machines. And like every riser, it lifts the built-in keyboard along with the screen, so long sessions still call for an external keyboard and mouse at elbow height.

Performance in Daily Use

Owner feedback on this stand is unusually consistent. The set height holds without drift in daily use, the open frame keeps air moving under the laptop, and the stand folds and unfolds in seconds with no tools or loose parts. Owners who use the space underneath for a mouse or notebook count it as part of the appeal.

Two recurring owner notes are worth knowing before you buy. First, stability is best at the lower and middle settings on a flat, solid desk; at the top setting, vigorous typing is more noticeable. Second, owners who type on the raised laptop itself feel the compromise quickly — the owners who stay happy long-term are the ones who pair it with an external keyboard and mouse, which is the setup ergonomic guidance recommends anyway.

Who This Is For

Anyone with a laptop up to 15.6 inches who wants to raise the screen toward eye level at a desk — and wants the stand to fold flat when it is not in use. It fits hybrid workers who move between a home desk and other workspaces especially well. If you need fine height adjustment instead of preset steps, look at the Lamicall adjustable stand; if your laptop is a heavy 16-inch machine, look at a stand rated above 10 lbs instead.

Bottom Line

The Amazon Basics laptop stand is the simple default for laptops up to 15.6 inches: six preset heights, an open airflow design, and a fold-flat aluminum frame, with its limits — preset steps and a 10 lb ceiling — stated plainly. Pair it with an external keyboard and mouse and it does the posture job most desks need.
